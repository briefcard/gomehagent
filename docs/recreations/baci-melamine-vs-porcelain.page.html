Melamine vs Porcelain Dinnerware: Which Table Is It For?

Baci Milano · Buying guide · September 2026

Melamine for the tables that get knocked, carried and left in the sun; porcelain for the tables that are the occasion. That is the whole answer, and if you set both kinds of table, the right buy is one pattern in both materials. Below: where each wins, a side-by-side, real prices from our own shelves, and how to choose by the table you actually set.

The short version

  • Melamine is light, resistant to breakage and dishwasher safe. It is the outdoor, poolside, everyday-with-kids plate. Never in the microwave.
  • Porcelain has the weight and the ring of a proper dinner. It goes in the microwave and the oven. It can chip.
  • Melamine no longer has to look like a picnic: our patterns are printed the same on both.

What you are actually choosing between

Porcelain is fired clay: kaolin and feldspar, vitrified hard, glazed, decorated. It is the material of the formal table for three hundred years because it holds heat, takes fine decoration and has a density you feel in the hand.

Melamine is a resin, moulded under heat and pressure into a plate that is hard, light and very difficult to break. For decades it was the material of school canteens and camping boxes, which is why "melamine" still sounds like a compromise. The material never was; the designs were.

So the question is not "which is better". It is "which table". A candlelit dinner and a lunch by the pool are two tables with two sets of demands, and each material answers one of them well.

Where porcelain wins

  • The occasion. Weight, a glaze that catches candlelight, the small sound of a knife on a plate that means dinner. No other material does this.
  • Heat. Porcelain goes in the microwave and, for most pieces, the oven. A plate warmed before the food arrives is a porcelain habit.
  • Longevity. Kept away from the edge of the counter, a porcelain set outlives the table it sits on. The glaze does not dull.

The cost of those virtues is the one everybody knows: it chips, and when it breaks it breaks completely. A porcelain set is bought for the tables where nobody is running.

Where melamine wins

  • Anywhere but the microwave. The terrace, the pool, the boat, the beach house, the garden lunch with children under the table. Our Portofino set is made for exactly that: lightweight, resistant to breakage, dishwasher safe.
  • The carry. Eighteen pieces of melamine weigh a fraction of eighteen pieces of porcelain. Stacks go out to the table and back in one trip.
  • The price of a whole table. A complete service for six in melamine costs less than the same service in porcelain, from the same maker, in the same pattern. Ours: $640 against $920.

What melamine will not do: take heat. Never in the microwave, never in the oven, and hot food goes on it, not into it. Turmeric and saffron can leave a faint colour over time; a soak lifts it.

Why melamine looked cheap, and why it no longer has to

The canteen plate was thin, plain white or a single flat colour, and stamped rather than designed. The material took the blame. In fact melamine takes a printed pattern as well as anything — the problem was only that nobody bothered. When the same coral-and-shell pattern that goes on a porcelain dinner plate goes on a melamine one, at the same size, in a set with its own serving bowl and tray, the terrace table stops looking like a picnic and starts looking like the dining room moved outside.

So when you compare the two materials, compare the good version of each. A cheap melamine plate against a fine porcelain one is not a comparison of materials; it is a comparison of budgets.

And stoneware, or "ceramic"?

Most of what is sold as ceramic dinnerware is stoneware: clay fired at a lower temperature than porcelain, so heavier, a little more porous, and more likely to chip at the rim. It is the material of the everyday indoor plate in a lot of homes. Against melamine it has porcelain's weaknesses without porcelain's refinement; against porcelain it is heavier and less fine. If you are choosing between all three, the question is the same one: which table.

How to choose, by the table you set

  • Most meals are outside, or there are children, or both: melamine, and buy the complete set so nothing is missing when eight turn up.
  • Dinner is the event and the table is indoors: porcelain, and warm the plates.
  • You set both tables: one pattern in both materials. The eye stops noticing which is which; the hands do not.
  • You are buying a first proper set: porcelain for the room you eat in every night; add the melamine the first summer you carry it outside.
  • It is a gift, a registry, a second home: melamine. It arrives intact, it survives the guests, and it looks like the photograph.

What to look for in a melamine set

  • A complete service, not a stack of plates. Dinner plates, soup bowls and dessert plates for six, so the table is set from the first course to the last. Ours are 28, 23 and 21.5 centimetres.
  • Serveware in the same pattern. A serving bowl and a tray that match the plates are what make an outdoor table look considered rather than assembled. Portofino has both.
  • Weight in the hand. Good melamine is light but not flimsy; a plate should feel solid at the rim.
  • The pattern printed for the plate, not shrunk from a catalogue: colour right to the edge, the motif sized for the round.

Care, so both last

Melamine: the dishwasher is fine (our Portofino set is made for it); the microwave and the oven are not, ever. Serve hot food on it rather than heating food in it. Cut on a board, not the plate. Between meals, out of direct sun.

Porcelain: microwave and oven as the maker allows; for a decorated piece, read its note before the first wash. Stack with a little care, and it will be the set your children argue over.

Questions people ask

Is melamine dinnerware safe to eat from?

Yes, for serving. Melamine plates are made for food and used in homes and restaurants everywhere. The one rule is heat: never microwave food on melamine and never put it in the oven.

Can melamine go in the microwave?

No. Heat is the one thing it does not take. Warm the food, then plate it.

Is melamine dishwasher safe?

Ours is. The Portofino melamine set is dishwasher safe; it is one of the reasons it is the outdoor set.

Does porcelain always chip?

Not always, and not soon, but it can, and when a porcelain plate falls it is gone. That is the honest price of the material that owns the formal table.
